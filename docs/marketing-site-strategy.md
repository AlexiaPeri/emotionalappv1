# Emerge marketing site strategy

The public product site lives in `site/`. It is separate from the app prototype (`index.html`) and the private writing studio (`writing.html`).

## Audiences

1. Potential users who need to understand the experience quickly.
2. Entrepreneurship programmes evaluating the clarity and relevance of the concept.
3. Potential partners and early investors looking for a differentiated mechanism, rigorous positioning and an honest product stage.

## Narrative

1. **Offer:** a voice-led practice for emotional release.
2. **Need:** distraction is readily available when emotions rise; direct, contained practices are not.
3. **Vision:** make emotional release as available as distraction.
4. **Practice:** focus on the feeling, move out of explanation, go deeper and let the body participate.
5. **Product:** a quiet interface designed to make the screen disappear during practice.
6. **Differentiation:** less interpretation, more direct contact with present emotional experience.
7. **Evidence:** related mechanisms are supported by research; the Emerge practice remains a product hypothesis to validate.

## Messaging Rules

- The public site should establish the value and felt logic of Emerge before explaining its technical repetition loop.
- Do not demonstrate the call-and-response interaction or use a sample sentence on the public site.
- Repetition may be named later as a means of sustaining focus, but it is not the first explanation of why the practice matters.
- Use language around focus, contact, feeling, movement, depth and leaving the thinking mind.
- Mention the Meisner repetition practice as an important inspiration without implying that Emerge is Meisner training.
- Prefer positive statements. Use negation only when the contrast is genuinely meaningful, such as distraction versus emotional contact.
- Describe emotional release as focused contact and movement, not catharsis, venting or acting out.
- Preserve enough mystery that the guided first-use experience can teach the exact practice in context.

## Evidence boundary

The site must never imply that studies of affect labeling, suppression, movement or emotion regulation prove that Emerge itself is effective. The association between emotion-regulation difficulties and aggression is correlational and does not show that Emerge prevents violence. Research on catharsis also cautions against equating emotional release with simply venting anger. Product outcomes require dedicated beta research. Emerge is positioned as a wellness practice, not a medical device, diagnosis or replacement for professional care.
