# Preliminary name research - 1 August 2026

## Executive recommendation

Change the product name before a public beta or any meaningful marketing investment.

`Emerge` is elegant and aligned with the product, but it is too crowded to be a strong worldwide app brand. Multiple apps already use the exact name, including products in Health & Fitness. Trademark searches also return a large number of results, including live rights and filings in classes relevant to software, education and health-related services.

This does not establish that using `Emerge` is unlawful. Trademark conflicts depend on the exact goods and services, territories, earlier rights and likelihood of confusion. It does mean that the name carries avoidable legal, App Store discovery and brand differentiation risk.

Because the product is still being validated, the proportionate approach is:

1. Select two or three distinctive candidates.
2. Ask an EU/France trademark professional to perform a similarity clearance covering at least the EU, UK and US.
3. Reserve the chosen domain and App Store name after clearance.
4. File first in the launch territory; extend internationally only when traction justifies the cost.

Do not rename the app code or visual assets until a candidate has passed step 2.

## Apple findings

Apple asks developers to choose a unique name, avoid names protected by third-party trademarks, and keep the App Store name between 2 and 30 characters. Apple also recommends a name that is distinctive, memorable and easy to spell.

## Current preference

For positioning, **Feel It Through** is the strongest working direction: it tells the truth about the practice and the preliminary exact-name results are comparatively clean.

For a more ownable, elegant one-word brand, **Emovela** is the strongest creative direction, subject to a professional similarity opinion concerning `EMOVIA`.

The next decision should therefore be between two naming strategies, not yet between final names:

- **Descriptive and immediate:** `Feel It Through`
- **Distinctive and brandable:** `Emovela` or a further coined-name round

### English-first naming requirement

The worldwide launch is primarily English-facing. A candidate should therefore pass a stricter spoken-brand test:

- an English speaker should pronounce it correctly on first sight;
- an English speaker should spell it correctly after hearing it once;
- it should not depend on a silent final `e`, an unexpected doubled consonant, or an accent mark;
- the simpler spelling should not already belong to a prominent app or consumer brand;
- it should sound natural in a sentence such as `I use ___` or `Download ___`.

This changes the assessment of two candidates. `Mure` has the attractive English pronunciation `myoor`, but it must be **eliminated** as a worldwide app candidate. An exact [EU word mark MURE](https://www.trademarkelite.com/europe/trademark/trademark-detail/019203265/MURE), application `019203265`, is registered for directly overlapping class 9 goods including mobile apps, mindfulness-training software and mobile applications for managing personal habits. An exact [MURE iPhone app](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/mure-%E4%BB%B2%E9%96%93%E3%81%A8%E7%9B%AE%E6%A8%99%E3%82%92%E9%81%94%E6%88%90%E3%81%97%E3%82%88%E3%81%86/id6759218437) is also available in Japan, and `mure.app` is actively used by a software company. This is a materially stronger conflict than a merely registered domain. `Lomi` is already an active Lifestyle app and consumer appliance brand, with both primary domains registered and software-related trademark rights.

## Limits of this research

This is a preliminary risk screen, not legal advice or a guarantee of availability. Search tools can be incomplete or delayed. Trademark assessment includes phonetic, visual and conceptual similarity, not only exact matches. App Store search results also do not guarantee that a name can be reserved in App Store Connect.
