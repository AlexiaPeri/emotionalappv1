# Preliminary name research - 1 August 2026

## Executive recommendation

Change the product name before a public beta or any meaningful marketing investment.

`Emerge` is elegant and aligned with the product, but it is too crowded to be a strong worldwide app brand. Multiple apps already use the exact name, including products in Health & Fitness. Trademark searches also return a large number of results, including live rights and filings in classes relevant to software, education and health-related services.

This does not establish that using `Emerge` is unlawful. Trademark conflicts depend on the exact goods and services, territories, earlier rights and likelihood of confusion. It does mean that the name carries avoidable legal, App Store discovery and brand differentiation risk.

Because the product is still being validated, the proportionate approach is:

1. Select two or three distinctive candidates.
2. Ask an EU/France trademark professional to perform a similarity clearance covering at least the EU, UK and US.
3. Reserve the chosen domain and App Store name after clearance.
4. File first in the launch territory; extend internationally only when traction justifies the cost.

Do not rename the app code or visual assets until a candidate has passed step 2.

## Apple findings

Apple asks developers to choose a unique name, avoid names protected by third-party trademarks, and keep the App Store name between 2 and 30 characters. Apple also recommends a name that is distinctive, memorable and easy to spell.

Official references:

- [App Review Guidelines - section 2.3.7](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- [Creating your product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- [App information reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-information)
- [Add a new app](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/create-an-app-record/add-a-new-app/)

## Trademark findings

The search used TMview, coordinated by the EUIPO, plus official USPTO records and public App Store results. TMview is a screening tool and does not replace the official registers or a professional clearance opinion.

- A TMview search for `EMERGE` returned 789 records. Of these, 321 included at least one of classes 9, 41, 42, 44 or 45. The result set includes exact and composite marks, live and ended records.
- A live EU application for the word mark `EMERGE`, application `019361733`, was filed on 9 May 2026 in classes 9, 41, 42 and 44 by Verein EMERGE. The organisation concerns emergency-medicine education, so its activity is not identical, but the mark and relevant classes overlap.
- A US word mark `EMERGE`, registration `7,965,101`, is registered for weight-loss and wellness-related services in class 44. [Official USPTO status record](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=98853466&caseSearchType=US_APPLICATION&caseType=DEFAULT&searchType=statusSearch)
- Another US `EMERGE` registration, `3,517,690`, appears in software-related classes 9 and 42 in TMview.

## Filtered candidate directions

These are creative candidates that survived a preliminary exact-name screen. They are not legally cleared names. Similarity searches, unregistered rights, company names and local-language meanings still need professional review.

| Candidate | Why it fits | Preliminary findings | Main reservation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Feel It Through** | Describes staying with a feeling until it moves; directly reflects the product mechanism | No exact TMview mark or exact App Store title found in the checked results; no `.com` or `.app` registration returned by RDAP on 1 Aug 2026 | An ordinary English phrase is harder to own and protect; needs native-speaker testing |
| **Emovela** | Coined, elegant and suggestive of emotion in movement without making a medical claim | No exact TMview mark or indexed exact App Store title found; no `.com` or `.app` registration returned by RDAP on 1 Aug 2026 | Must be compared professionally with the registered French mark `EMOVIA` in classes 9 and 42 |
| **Emovelle** | Softer, premium-sounding variation that can preserve the current visual identity | No exact TMview mark or exact App Store title found; no `.com` or `.app` registration returned by RDAP on 1 Aug 2026 | Also requires a similarity review against `EMOVIA`; may sound more feminine in French |
| **Voiara** | Suggests voice and air while remaining a coined brand | No exact TMview mark or exact App Store title found | Meaning and pronunciation are less immediate; `.com` is already registered |
| **Feel It Move** | Clear, active and very close to the product promise | No exact TMview mark or exact App Store title found in the checked results | The phrase is already used in music and wellness wording; `.com` is registered, so distinctiveness is weaker |

Candidates eliminated during screening include `Emovere`, `Emovia`, `Alivra`, `Veluna`, `Ovelia`, `Orevia`, `Fluere`, `Solura`, `Sentiara`, `Inner Weather` and `Unhold` because of existing marks, apps or material commercial use.

## Current preference

For positioning, **Feel It Through** is the strongest working direction: it tells the truth about the practice and the preliminary exact-name results are comparatively clean.

For a more ownable, elegant one-word brand, **Emovela** is the strongest creative direction, subject to a professional similarity opinion concerning `EMOVIA`.

The next decision should therefore be between two naming strategies, not yet between final names:

- **Descriptive and immediate:** `Feel It Through`
- **Distinctive and brandable:** `Emovela` or a further coined-name round

## Limits of this research

This is a preliminary risk screen, not legal advice or a guarantee of availability. Search tools can be incomplete or delayed. Trademark assessment includes phonetic, visual and conceptual similarity, not only exact matches. App Store search results also do not guarantee that a name can be reserved in App Store Connect.
