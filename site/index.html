The need

We have endless ways to avoid a feeling. Very few ways to stay with one.

When emotion rises, the easiest reflex is often to scroll, distract, explain or push it away. Emerge offers a different next step: a contained practice that turns attention back toward the feeling and the body.

It is not another place to consume advice. It is a place to do something with what is already here.

The Emerge loop

A conversation stripped back to what matters.

Emerge mirrors the user’s own words. It does not analyse, advise or rewrite the experience.

You “I feel overwhelmed.”
Emerge “You feel overwhelmed.”
  1. 01Feel

    Bring attention out of the story and into the body.

  2. 02Say

    Use one simple sentence. No explanation is required.

  3. 03Hear

    The app reflects the sentence back, changing only the pronouns.

  4. 04Repeat

    Stay with the experience and let the words change when the feeling changes.

The experience

Designed for the moment the screen should disappear.

The interface becomes deliberately quiet during practice. Voice, movement and attention take over.

Why it is different

Less interpretation.
More contact.

Your words remain yours
No generative AI reframes, diagnoses or improves what the user says.
Voice keeps the practice alive
The loop responds in the moment, without turning the experience into journaling or chat.
Repetition interrupts explanation
The same phrase becomes an anchor for attention until the felt experience naturally changes.
The body is part of the interface
Movement is invited throughout; the aim is not to keep the user looking at a screen.

Scientific foundations

Built from credible mechanisms. Tested with honest questions.

Research supports several mechanisms present in Emerge. The specific voice-repetition experience is a product hypothesis and will require its own user research and validation.

01

Putting feelings into words

In an fMRI study, affect labeling was associated with lower amygdala responses to negative emotional images and greater activity in the right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex.

Lieberman et al., 2007 ↗
02

Staying in contact instead of suppressing

Experimental research has linked expressive suppression with cognitive costs, including poorer memory, and with increased cardiovascular activation during suppression.

Richards & Gross, 1999 ↗
03

Language while approaching a feeling

In one experiment, pairing affect labeling with exposure produced a greater reduction in skin-conductance response at one-week follow-up than exposure alone.

Kircanski et al., 2012 ↗
04

Emotion is embodied

Research and clinical literature increasingly examine movement, proprioception and interoception as meaningful parts of emotional experience and regulation.

Shafir, 2016 ↗

Important: Emerge is being developed as a wellness practice, not a medical device or substitute for professional mental health care. Research on related mechanisms does not establish the effectiveness of the app itself.

The vision

Make emotional release as available as distraction.

Most digital tools begin after the emotional moment: tracking it, explaining it or offering content about it. Emerge is designed for the moment itself.

The ambition is simple: when someone instinctively reaches for their phone, Emerge can become a different reflex — direct, private and embodied.
